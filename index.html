POWER THAT SERVES PEOPLE

Why clean electricity matters

✳
01

Cleaner air at generation

Wind and PV generate electricity without burning fuel on site. When they displace fossil generation, they can reduce combustion-related air pollution and CO₂.

02

Less fuel dependence

Wind and sunlight are local energy flows. Using them can diversify electricity supply and reduce exposure to fuel-price and delivery swings.

03

Build a system that works

Sun and wind vary with weather and time. Strong grids, storage, flexible demand, and other generation help turn variable output into reliable service.

Benefits depend on location, what generation is displaced, materials, siting, and how the grid is operated. IEA: emissions, air pollution, and water impacts ↗ · IEA: integration and flexibility ↗

07 / THE SCIENCE

What changed?

✳

How does the Rankine cycle work?

The pump raises liquid-water pressure. The boiler adds heat to make steam. Steam expands through a turbine, producing shaft work. A generator converts most shaft work to electricity. The condenser rejects heat so the water can return to the pump. The cycle model uses Q̇in = Ẇturbine − Ẇpump + Q̇out.

What do the controls mean?

Boiler pressure: the high-side pressure before expansion. Steam temperature: turbine-inlet temperature; above saturation this is superheat. Mass flow: steam passing each second. Turbine efficiency: actual enthalpy drop divided by ideal isentropic enthalpy drop. Condenser pressure: turbine exhaust pressure; lower pressure generally increases the available expansion.

How should I read steam quality?

Quality is the vapor mass fraction of a saturated liquid–vapor mixture. It is defined only inside the two-phase region. A “superheated” exhaust has quality undefined, not greater than 100%. Low quality means more liquid droplets and can increase turbine-blade erosion risk.
