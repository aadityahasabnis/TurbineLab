01 / THE BIG PICTURE

A power plant is a loop.

Heat makes steam. Steam turns a turbine. The turbine turns a generator. Then the steam cools back into water so the cycle can start again.

Aha

The turbine does not “make energy.” It converts part of the steam’s energy into rotation. The generator converts much of that shaft work into electricity.

03 / WHERE THE ENERGY GOES

Every output has a boundary.

Start with heat supplied to the cycle. Some becomes net shaft work; the rest leaves as heat from the condenser.

ONE CYCLE ENERGY BUDGET BALANCED
Heat added→net work + heat rejectedQ̇in = Ẇnet + Q̇out

Generator loss is a second, nested split of shaft work. Do not add it again to the first heat-input split.

TURBINE
Ẇt = ṁ(h1 − h2)

Mass flow multiplied by the enthalpy drop gives turbine power.

PUMP
Ẇp ≈ ṁvΔP

Pressurizing liquid needs work. In this cycle it is small, but it is counted.

EFFICIENCY
η = Ẇnet / Q̇in

Net cycle work divided by heat supplied to the working fluid.

Open the full calculation chain

1 · Estimate the inlet: use saturated vapor properties at boiler pressure, then add a simple superheat correction.

h₁ ≈ hᵍ + cₚ(T₁ − Tₛₐₜ)

2 · Find an ideal outlet: expand at constant entropy to condenser pressure.

s₂ₛ = s₁  →  h₂ₛ from condenser-pressure properties

3 · Apply turbine efficiency:

h₂ = h₁ − ηₜ(h₁ − h₂ₛ)

4 · Complete the cycle balance:

Ẇnet = Ẇt − Ẇp   ·   Q̇in = ṁ(h₁ − h₄)   ·   Q̇out = Q̇in − Ẇnet

Steam quality is calculated from h₂ only when the outlet is inside the saturated liquid–vapor region.

06 / QUESTIONS, DEFINITIONS & SOURCES

Stay curious. Check the model.

Use these answers to guide discussion. The full property calculations used by real engineers are more detailed than this classroom simulator.

Why doesn't higher pressure always mean more power?

Power depends on energy extracted per kilogram and steam flow. Pressure changes both the inlet and exhaust states. In this model, high pressure can also lower exit quality. The exact curve is model-dependent.

Why can the quality display say “DRY”?

Quality describes a saturated liquid–vapor mixture only. Superheated vapor is outside that region, so calling it 100% quality would be misleading.

Why might a low quality concern engineers?

A wetter exhaust contains more liquid droplets. In real turbines, droplets can erode blades. The simulation's red line at 85% is an educational alert, not a universal design limit.

Why does more flow raise power but not efficiency?

At fixed thermodynamic conditions, both heat input and work output grow in proportion to mass flow. Their ratio therefore remains about the same.

What does “isentropic” mean?

It describes an idealized expansion at constant entropy. Turbine efficiency compares the real enthalpy drop with this ideal reference.

QUICK GLOSSARY

Useful language

Enthalpy · h

Energy per unit mass used to track energy changes in flowing steam.

Entropy · s

A thermodynamic property used to describe the ideal constant-entropy expansion.

Superheat

Steam temperature above the boiling temperature at the same pressure.

Specific volume · v

Volume occupied by one kilogram of the working fluid.

Net shaft work

Turbine work after subtracting the work required by the pump.

Sankey diagram

A flow diagram where band width represents the amount of energy.

Megawatt · MW

One million joules of energy transferred every second.

ⓘ

What this simulator leaves out

TurbineLab uses rounded saturation values, simple superheat and pump equations, a fixed generator efficiency, and schematic diagrams. It does not model supercritical steam, reheat, turbine stages, or each power source's full plant. Use the results to explore trends, not to design or operate equipment.

See the model assumptions
  • The property table is sparse and linearly interpolated; it is not a complete IAPWS calculation.
  • Superheated steam uses approximate heat capacity. Real properties vary with pressure and temperature.
  • Boiler pressure is limited to 20 MPa; supercritical operation is outside this model.
  • Pump work uses constant liquid specific volume. Generator efficiency is fixed at 98%.
  • RPM is mapped from power for the animation, not calculated from turbine design or grid speed.
  • The T–s dome, P–v path, and plant scenario cards are teaching illustrations.
  • The 85% wet-steam alert is a classroom threshold, not a universal operating limit.
